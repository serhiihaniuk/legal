# Batch 129: extraordinary review of an administrative decision

Status: preparation only. No module, commentary, registry, shared renderer or test has been changed. This record is not acceptance of the existing module or a future rewrite.

Prepared on 23 September 2026 using the legal-explanatory-writing skill. The proposed legal review is limited to this module. It does not change the corpus edition or the atlas baseline.

## Reader question and current gap

The reader needs to understand which defect can justify reopening a completed case, which defect can justify invalidity, and why a proven defect sometimes still cannot remove the old decision. A timely application, an order reopening proceedings and a decision repealing the original act are three different events.

The owned future product file would be `app/data/legal-library/learning/modules/kpa/extraordinary.ts`, stable ID `extraordinary`, order `14`. The current module has layered directions and two short lesson paragraphs. Its non-participation example ends with instructions to check the ground, knowledge date and authority. It does not supply a completed application, issued procedural act or final form of relief. The rewrite should replace that structure with connected explanations and a completed example, while retaining the distinctions already present.

Specific gaps in the present explanation:

- The difference between admissibility of reopening, investigation after reopening and the outcomes under Article 151 is not explained through an actual sequence.
- The month for the party's request and the five- or ten-year limit on repealing the decision are mentioned without showing that they answer different questions.
- The authority receiving the request under Article 148 is not necessarily the authority resolving reopening under Article 150.
- The grounds in Articles 145a, 145aa and 145b need their different events starting the month. Non-participation under Article 145 § 1 point 4 also has its own knowledge event under Article 148 § 2.
- Invalidity needs a clear separation between the ten-year/irreversible-effects bar on the relief and the thirty-year bar on initiating proceedings.
- Articles 154 and 155 require more than the phrase "their own conditions". Acquired rights, consent, the protected interests and special-law restrictions must be explained.
- Articles 161–163a are in the panel but barely explained. They should receive a bounded orientation without turning the article into an account of every special permit regime.
- A private evidence table is suggested as an attachment without explaining that it is a working aid, not a universally required document.
- Do not turn "rażące naruszenie prawa" into a supposedly complete statutory test composed of adjectives. The code names the ground; a detailed judicial test would require separately selected current judgments.

The current definition of invalidity speaks only about a final decision. For the rewrite, explain the selected review of final decisions through Article 16, but do not assert that the literal text of Article 156 universally confines invalidity proceedings to final decisions. That broader admissibility question requires additional interpretation and is unnecessary for the selected example.

### Reopening: exact passages to retain

- Page 31, Article 145 § 1: eight grounds. False evidence, crime, exclusion of the worker/authority, non-participation without the party's fault, previously existing material facts/evidence unknown to the issuer, lack of the required position of another authority, a preliminary question resolved differently by the competent authority/court, and a changed or repealed predicate decision/judgment are distinct grounds. Sections 2–3 qualify reliance on the first two grounds before or without the usual prior finding.
- Page 31, Articles 145a, 145aa and 145b: the request month starts respectively from entry into force of the relevant Constitutional Tribunal judgment, publication of the operative part of the relevant CJEU judgment in the Official Journal of the EU, or finality of the relevant equal-treatment court judgment. Keep each substantive connection to the administrative decision, not merely the existence of a judgment.
- Page 32, Article 147: reopening may be ex officio or on request, but Article 145 § 1 point 4 and Articles 145a–145b require the party's request.
- Page 32, Article 148: submit the request to the authority which issued the first-instance decision. The ordinary month runs from learning the circumstance forming the ground; for non-participation, it runs from learning of the decision. Article 57 § 3 on page 14 governs a period expressed in months. Do not replace a month with thirty days.
- Page 32, Article 149: an order reopens proceedings and provides the basis for investigating the grounds and deciding the merits. Refusal to reopen is also an order, with a complaint under § 4. The opening order itself is not the decision under Article 151.
- Page 32, Article 150: normally the authority which issued the last-instance decision is competent. If the ground concerns that authority's activity, the higher authority decides on reopening and appoints the authority to handle the matters in Article 149 § 2. The minister/SKO exception in § 3 must remain visible when this rule is explained.
- Page 32, Article 151: after the investigation, absence of a reopening ground produces a decision refusing repeal. A ground allowing relief produces repeal and a new decision on the merits. Where Article 146 prevents repeal, § 2 requires a declaration that the challenged decision was issued in violation of law and identification of the circumstances preventing repeal. Section 3 excludes tacit disposal.
- Page 32, Article 146: for Article 145 § 1 points 1–2, repeal is barred after ten years from service or announcement; for points 3–8 and Articles 145a–145b, after five years. Section 2 separately bars repeal when the only possible new decision would correspond in substance to the old one. These restrictions must not be mistaken for the party's month under Article 148.
- Page 32, Article 152: the competent authority suspends execution ex officio or on request when the circumstances indicate probable repeal. The statutory verb is mandatory once the condition exists. A reopening request or the opening order is not itself automatic suspension. Section 2 provides a complaint in the matter of suspension, with the minister/SKO exception.

### Invalidity and the 2021 transition

- Page 33, Article 156 § 1: retain the seven distinct grounds. A decision concerning a case already disposed of by another final decision or tacitly is point 3. Initial permanent unenforceability, criminal consequences of execution and a defect to which a special rule attaches nullity are separate grounds. Mere disagreement with the evidence is not an additional ground.
- Page 33, Article 156 § 2: the ten-year period from service/announcement applies to the grounds in § 1; irreversible legal effects are a separate bar. Practical expense or difficulty is not, merely by its name, a statutory finding of irreversible legal effects.
- Page 33, Article 157: competent higher authority, or the issuing minister/SKO itself; initiation on request or ex officio. Do not derive the competent authority from Article 148, which concerns reopening.
- Page 33, Article 158: a decision resolves the matter and tacit disposal is unavailable. If an invalidity ground is established but Article 156 § 2 prevents invalidity, § 2 gives the limited declaration of violation and its preventing circumstances. After thirty years from service/announcement, § 3 prevents initiation. Age alone does not establish an invalidity ground.
- Page 33, Article 159: execution must be suspended when a ground from Article 156 § 1 is probable. The complaint wording in § 2 concerns an order suspending execution. Do not mechanically copy the differently worded Article 152 § 2.
- Amending act DU/2021/1491: <https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2021/1491/text/O/D20211491.pdf>. The entire one-page original was read live. Metadata <https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2021/1491> reports in force, promulgation 16 August 2021 and entry into force 16 September 2021. Article 1 introduced the present ten-year wording and thirty-year rule. Article 2(1) applies the new wording to the specified invalidity proceedings already pending without a final decision/order. Article 2(2) ends by operation of law the specified pending proceedings initiated more than thirty years after service/announcement. Do not present this as a universal termination of all old cases or apply it to reopening.

### Other changes to a final decision

- Page 32, Article 154: no party acquired a right; the issuing authority may repeal or change at any time when the social interest or the party's legitimate interest supports it. The form is a decision.
- Page 33, Article 155: where a party acquired a right, retain consent, absence of opposition in special provisions, and social interest or the party's legitimate interest. These are not a general mechanism for replacing a permit's purpose or bypassing a special statutory change procedure. Article 155a excludes tacit disposal for Articles 154–155.
- Pages 33–34, Article 161: the minister's exceptional power addresses the specified danger or serious harm which cannot otherwise be removed/prevented. The wojewoda's § 2 power has its particular scope. If compensation is mentioned, state only the defined actual-loss claim and its statutory conditions/time rule from §§ 3–4. Do not import a full civil-liability account or imply that every declaration under Article 151 or 158 awards damages.
- Page 33–34, Article 162: distinguish expiry because of mootness with the additional statutory/public-or-party-interest condition, expiry after failure of a condition, and repeal for failure to perform the prescribed acts within the specified time. The issuer of the first-instance decision acts in decision form.
- Page 34, Articles 163–163a: special provisions may authorize other changes of decisions granting rights, on their own conditions. No tacit disposal under Articles 161–163. Articles 153 and 160 are repealed; do not fabricate a current mechanism for them.

## Proposed article structure

1. **Що можна змінити після остаточного рішення.** Explain finality and the specific ground, distinguish ordinary disagreement from reopening, invalidity and a permitted change under Articles 154–155. Keep the special-law boundary beside this explanation.
2. **Wznowienie: підстава, строк і компетентний орган.** Define the grounds through their mechanisms, including the old-fact/new-document distinction, special judgment grounds and non-participation. Explain the request month and the two authority roles.
3. **Від відкриття провадження до рішення про результат.** Explain Articles 149–152, the five/ten-year limits and same-substance limitation, then use the completed example below.
4. **Nieważność: вада рішення та межі її усунення.** Explain Article 156 grounds, Articles 157–159, ten years versus thirty years, irreversible legal effects and the bounded 2021 transition.
5. **Зміна, скасування або припинення дії з інших причин.** Explain acquired rights/consent under 154–155 and a concise 161–163a orientation. End with the actual operative result: opening, repeal, limited violation declaration or expiry must not be treated as the same result.

No fixed section count is mandatory. Keep all material qualifications in rendered prose, not only claims or source notes. A short final reminder can distinguish a new decision from its service and subsequent finality, without reproducing the whole ordinary-appeal or court module.

## Proposed completed example

Use an expressly simplified general KPA case, not a real residence or professional-qualification regime. The assumed substantive condition is that the applicant had a named qualification by the date relevant to the original case. That condition is a declared premise of the hypothetical, not a newly asserted rule of immigration law. No personal identities, file number, actual certificate number, address or signature should appear.

Selected facts and completed events:

1. A refusal was issued on 15 June 2020 and duly served on 18 June 2020. It became final without an appeal. The sole disputed substantive fact was the qualification at the relevant earlier date. No extraordinary proceeding remained pending before July 2025.
2. On 3 August 2026 the applicant first learned of a located examination protocol made in 2019, and obtained its copy with an archive covering letter. The protocol existed before the refusal and was unknown to the issuing authority. It records the qualification already obtained at the relevant date. The new covering letter supplies provenance and the discovery event; its 2026 date is not the claimed old circumstance or the date on which the qualification arose.
3. On 24 August 2026 the applicant actually lodged the signed reopening request at the first-instance authority in the permitted paper form. A receipt on the retained copy confirms that event. The request identifies Article 145 § 1 point 5, the old record, materiality, the knowledge date and the original decision. The month under Article 148 ends on 3 September 2026, so this filing is timely.
4. The authority competent under Article 150 § 1 issued an order reopening proceedings on 28 August 2026. Stipulate that the original authority also issued the last-instance decision and that the selected ground does not concern its activity within Article 150 § 2. No higher-authority designation is invented.
5. The investigation confirmed the old record's existence, the issuer's lack of knowledge and its materiality. On 15 September 2026 the authority issued a decision under Article 151 § 2 declaring that the original decision had been issued in violation of law. It identified the five-year bar in Article 146 § 1 as the reason repeal was unavailable. The new decision was duly served on 18 September 2026.

Do not turn the five-year point into another last-day exercise. More than six years have passed since the stipulated 2020 service. The month and the elapsed five-year limit are therefore visibly different without a disputed edge date. Do not claim the new 2026 decision is already final, that an appeal has been filed, that the original refusal was repealed, that the qualification itself creates a permit, or that compensation was awarded.

The existing map's payment example establishes an opening order. This example deliberately reaches a different, limited issued decision. The use of a hypothetical substantive condition must remain visible in the finished Ukrainian facts and Polish specimen note.
