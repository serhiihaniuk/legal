## Reader question and explanatory gaps

The page should answer what an issued decision or order actually determines, when it binds the authority or can be executed, and which defects can be completed, corrected or explained without replacing review of the result.

The legacy text names `ostateczna`, `prawomocna`, `wykonalna`, completion and correction but does not explain their separate conditions. It does not explain the cooperation required by Articles 106 and 106a, although both are in its live provision panel. It treats the content of a decision mainly as fields to find, rather than explaining how the operative result, evidence assessment and remedy instruction perform different functions. The distinction between a decision and a procedural order also needs the qualifications in Articles 123–126.

The rewrite should remove homework. Required compatibility fields can remain short and informative, while the full explanation belongs in subject-specific sections. Direct `lesson.articles[].reference` fields must contain authored legal text, not bare interpolation tokens. Use the existing typed helpers for all authored locators.

| KPA, page 24, Articles 104–105                          | A decision resolves the merits wholly or partly, or otherwise ends proceedings in an instance. Mandatory discontinuance for a wholly or partly objectless proceeding differs from discretionary discontinuance requested by the initiating party, with no opposition from other parties and no conflict with the social interest. | KPA, pages 24–25, Articles 106–106a                     | A position from another authority is required where a legal provision makes the decision depend on it. The ordinary position period is without delay, no later than two weeks from delivery of the request, unless a legal provision sets another period. The position takes the form of an appealable order. Section 3 requires the proved facts, relied-on evidence and reasons for denying other evidence credibility or force. Special provisions can add elements. | KPA, page 25, Articles 108–110                          | Immediate execution requires the specified necessity grounds. Exceptionally important party interest can involve security. A later grant of immediate execution uses an appealable order. Written service is ordinary; Article 109 § 2 refers to Article 14 § 2 for oral announcement. The authority's binding under Article 110 begins at service or announcement unless KPA provides otherwise. This is distinct from the date printed on the decision and from administrative finality.                                                                                                                                                     |
| KPA, page 25, Article 109 §§ 3–4                        | Attachments to a paper decision may be served on another durable information medium with consent expressed in writing or orally into the record. Explain preservation and reproducibility of the information if using the statutory term. This is not a general rule that a loose email attachment replaces service of the decision.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| KPA, pages 25–26, Article 111                           | The party's fourteen-day request concerns completion of the operative result or specified remedy information, or correction of that instruction. The authority has the corresponding fourteen-day ex officio power. Completion or refusal of completion takes the form of an order. Section 2 connects the new period for the specified remedy to service or announcement of the order under § 1b. Do not attribute that effect to every letter asking the authority to change something.                                                                                                                                                      |
| KPA, page 26, Articles 112–113                          | Incorrect instruction cannot harm the party who followed it. Article 113 § 1 concerns clerical, arithmetic and other obvious errors; § 2 concerns doubts about the content and requires a party's or enforcement authority's request. Correction and explanation take the form of orders and have the express complaint route in § 3. A disagreement with evidence assessment is not converted into an obvious typo by the title of the party's letter.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| KPA, page 28, Articles 123–126                          | Orders normally decide individual procedural questions rather than the merits, unless KPA says otherwise. Article 124 specifies content and the conditions requiring reasons. Article 125 distinguishes written service, oral announcement and the instruction/reasons for court complaint or `sprzeciw`. Article 126 applies the named decision provisions appropriately; it does not import every decision rule unchanged. | KPA, page 5, Article 16; pages 28–29, Articles 127–127a | Define administrative finality and `prawomocność` separately. Waiver has its statutory effect when the issuing authority receives the last party's waiver. A favourable result alone is not a sufficient description of all these mechanisms.                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| KPA, page 29, Articles 128–130                          | Ordinary appeal is through the issuing authority within fourteen days from service or announcement, with special-period exceptions. Detailed reasons are not a universal admissibility requirement. Execution before expiry, the effect of timely appeal, immediate-execution exceptions and the all-parties circumstances in § 4 remain distinct from finality.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| KPA, page 31, Articles 141–144                          | Separate complaint is available where the Code provides it; the ordinary period is seven days. Article 142 preserves challenge in an appeal from the decision for an order without a separate complaint. Filing a complaint does not itself stay the order, although the issuer may stay it where justified. Do not give every order a separate complaint or an automatic stay.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

## Proposed authored structure

Six sections would accommodate the existing scope without turning this page into the separate appeals manual:

1. **What the authority resolves.** Explain `decyzja`, an operative merits result, partial resolution and discontinuance. Define `postanowienie` by its role, with the statutory exception. Briefly place a required cooperating authority's position and the Article 106a meeting beside the main decision.
2. **How the result is supported.** Explain `rozstrzygnięcie`, factual and legal reasons, and `pouczenie`. Show why a list of attached documents is not an assessment of them. Explain the limited reasons-omission rules and the consequence of the specific Article 127 § 1a case.
3. **Issue, service, binding and execution.** Separate the printed date, service, Article 110 binding, administrative finality, judicial finality and execution. Explain Article 108's grounds and Article 130's boundaries. Keep the new durable-medium attachment rule and its transition concise but exact.
4. **Completion, correction and explanation.** Explain the three different objects of Articles 111 and 113 and the limited protection in Article 112. Tie the remedy clock to the correct statutory act. Include the completed comparison below here or as the module's final example.
5. **An order has its own content and review route.** Explain Articles 124–126, conditional reasons, delivery and appropriate application. Distinguish separate complaint, deferred challenge and execution. Preserve the current `sprzeciw` wording without inventing a universal right to that remedy.
6. **What the completed records establish.** Read the Polish comparison, calculate both periods and state only the completed procedural outcome. This may instead be the dedicated case-example block if a sixth section would repeat it.

The live panel already includes 104, 105, 106, 106a, 107–113 and 123–126. Preserve that full core. Supporting 14, 16, 57, 127, 127a, 128–130 and 141–144 can be typed inline references. Parent decides whether substantive treatment warrants additions to the panel; no mapping was edited here.

## Proposed completed comparison

This is a proposed separate fiction for authorship, not a report about actual clients. It has no residence-specific instrument, no minister/SKO reconsideration, no special appeal period and no invented judicial result. Both ordinary one-party proceedings began in June 2026. The party has no representative. Proper direct service and the dates below are established facts. Both decisions are adverse, so Article 127 § 1a does not apply. There is no waiver. The sample does not adjudicate the substantive right.

- Both decisions were issued on 6 August and properly served on 10 August 2026.
- In file A, the decision omits the ordinary appeal instruction. On 12 August the party physically lodges a signed request to complete that instruction. The issuing authority completes it by an Article 111 § 1b order issued on 17 August and served on 19 August. The operative refusal remains unchanged. The party physically lodges an appeal through the issuing authority on 1 September. Its fourteen-day period ends on 2 September.
- In file B, the decision contains the correct appeal instruction but misspells the city in the heading as `Wrocłąw` instead of `Wrocław`. The authority, party and decision are otherwise unambiguously identified; this typo changes no operative result, finding or instruction. The party requests correction on 12 August. The issuing authority corrects only that typo by an Article 113 § 1 order issued on 17 August and served on 19 August. The ordinary appeal against the original refusal is physically lodged on 21 August, before its unchanged 24 August endpoint.

The day of service is excluded. The relevant endpoints, 24 August and 2 September, are weekdays. The later order in B does not acquire Article 111 § 2's effect merely because both later documents are called `postanowienie`. That conclusion is a comparison of the express provisions and Article 129's ordinary clock, not a quotation of a separate sentence in Article 113. Keep any complaint against the correction order distinct from the appeal against the original decision.

## Exact document destinations

- `administrative-decision`: the complete issued refusal, including result, reasons and instruction. It is not the application or the residence card.
- `procedural-order`: the issued completion/correction order. Its exact statutory function must be named in the sentence; the generic guide does not make every order separately appealable.
- `administrative-appeal`: the actual party's appeal against the original decision. No claim that detailed professional grounds are universally required.
- `delivery-proof`: evidence set establishing service of each identified incoming decision/order. Its catalog kind is an evidence set, not one universal national certificate.
- `dispatch-proof`: evidence set for actual lodgement of each identified outgoing request/appeal. Here use the retained office acknowledgement, not an unselected electronic receipt.
- `procedural-complaint`: conditional discussion of the separate complaint against an Article 113 order, not a replacement for the original-decision appeal and not an extra selected document in the completed comparison.

No dedicated completion-request or correction-request guide was found in the current guide inventory. Keep those documents plainly named and use exact typed statutory references. Do not disguise them as an administrative appeal, a working case assessment or a generic evidence package. `case-file-index` can identify a working index only; it is not proof of the contents or service of the decision. `proceeding-certificate` is a special filing-certificate family and is not the destination for any order in this example.
