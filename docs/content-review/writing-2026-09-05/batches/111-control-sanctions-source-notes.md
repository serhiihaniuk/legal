## Foreign-employment act

Primary source: [current compiled Dz.U. 2025 poz. 621](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/621/text/U/D20250621Lj.pdf), 100 physical pages, editorial date 17.06.2026. The official PDF was reopened and the relevant passages read. The [official act API](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/621) was fetched directly. It returns status `obowiązujący` and `inForce: IN_FORCE`; these are distinct fields. Its listed amendments include 1794 effective 27.04.2026, 203 effective 05.03.2026, 473 effective 08.07.2026 and 734 effective 20.06.2026. No later amendment was listed in this check.

- Article 2 points 2–3, physical pages 3–4: the employer and worker definitions are not identical. Employer points 2(a–c) concern unlawful stay, incompatible stay and absence of a required instrument. Points 2(d–e) concern inconsistent work conditions with express exceptions for lawful changes or another basis. Point 2(f) preserves the corporate exception. Worker point 3 does not reproduce every employer-side violation. The article therefore does not infer worker illegality from a contract-copy failure.
- Article 6(1)(2), in the same current text, supplies the corporate category referred to in the written-contract exception. It is not treated as a general exemption from every corporate obligation.
- Article 17(1)(2), physical page 25: the current text permits transmission of the Polish contract through the contract system or its Polish copy through the foreigner-case system before entrusting work. The harvest-assistance contract has its own seven-day exception. The example is ordinary warehouse employment and does not use that exception.
- Article 17(1)(9) and (2), physical page 26: the requested-records duty has specified authorities and records, in Polish or translated into Polish. It is not an automatic universal upload. The separate corporate exceptions apply to the named board, company-affairs and procuration functions and specified duties. The selected employee is outside them.
- Article 84(1–2), physical page 73: illegal entrusting carries 3,000–50,000 zł; the separately defined worker offence has a fine not lower than 1,000 zł. No upper limit is invented from the latter paragraph alone.
- Article 84(6), physical page 74: the named failures, including article 17(1)(2), carry 1,000–3,000 zł. The hypothetical failure to transmit is discussed under this specific rule, not automatically under illegal entrusting. In the completed fiction, the allegation of that failure is disproved.
- The remainder of article 84 was also read on pages 73–74 to distinguish the other offence ranges and the limited per-foreigner minimum rules. They are not reproduced as an exhaustive sanctions list or generalized across every offence.
- Article 85, physical pages 74–75: its exception is restricted to article 84(1) in the named absence-of-valid-stay-document situation. It requires the duties under article 4(2)/(4), subject to knowledge of forgery, and social-insurance registration where legally required. The explanation preserves the cumulative conditions and does not promise blanket immunity.
- Article 86, physical page 75: the destination of fines and referral to KPW do not establish the general competence to inspect employers.

The author did not add work-permit revocation grounds, removal proceedings, limitation periods, criminal-offence advice or a full account of overlapping sanctions. The final paragraph simply preserves document identity: a protocol or its correction is not a permit-revocation or residence decision.

## PIP competence, protocol and objections

Primary source: [current PIP act, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1712 with amendments](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2024/1712/text/U/D20241712Lj.pdf), 60 physical pages, editorial date 31.07.2026. The PDF incorporates amendments through 2026/473. The [underlying act API, DU2007/589](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2007/589) was checked directly and lists 473 effective 08.07.2026 as its latest amendment, with earlier 160, 769, 620 and 368 also present.

- Article 10(1)(4), physical page 6: employment, other paid work and work performed by foreigners are within the specified PIP control remit. This statement does not make PIP the exclusive inspection body.
- Article 27, physical page 28: the controlled entity provides the requested material and information. The guide does not invent an invariant attachment bundle.
- Article 31(1–2), physical pages 28–30: protocol form, findings, relevant facts and attachments. The text also allows recording oral decisions and orders. The article therefore distinguishes the protocol's recording function without pretending an inspector cannot issue a separate operative measure during control.
- Article 31(4–5), physical page 30: objections are reasoned, written and submitted before signature within seven days from presentation of the protocol. Presentation, inspection commencement and refusal to sign are distinct events.
- Article 31(6–7), physical page 30: the inspector examines objections and changes or supplements the relevant portion if justified, documenting corrections. The fictional later correction follows this mechanism.
- Article 31(8–11), physical page 30: refusal to sign does not prevent lawful measures; copies and the possible official note where no irregularity is found have their own rules. No claim is made that every inspection must produce an adverse protocol.
- Article 33(1), physical page 31: distinct decisions, submissions/orders and other measures can follow within their legal scope. The article does not extend into the separate new employment-relationship determination procedure.
- Article 36(1–1a), physical page 35, was freshly reopened after independent review to define `wystąpienia` accurately as written submissions with post-control conclusions and their legal basis. They are not speeches or interchangeable with decisions. The same-page article 37(1) corroborates the separate mandate/court route; no additional procedural deadline was added from it.

The [original July reform, Dz.U. 2026 poz. 473](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/473/text/O/D20260473.pdf) was also read. Article 13, physical page 15, retains previous provisions for controls begun and unfinished before its entry into force. Its relevant commencement is 08.07.2026. The example explicitly begins its inspection on 24.08.2026; the later protocol date alone is not used to establish the transition.

[PIP's public inspection-preparation guidance](https://www.pip.gov.pl/dla-pracodawcow/niezbednik-pracodawcy/jak-przygotowac-sie-do-kontroli) was opened as supporting orientation. The current statute, not the broader webpage, supplies the operative protocol rules. The page was not accepted as a comprehensive account of every July reform or mandate ceiling.

## Fault, mandate and court referral

[Current Kodeks wykroczeń, Dz.U. 2025 poz. 734](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/734/text/U/D20250734Lj.pdf), 58 physical pages, editorial date 05.05.2026. Article 1 §2 on physical page 1 requires attributable fault at the time of the act. The [underlying act API, DU1971/114](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/1971/114) was checked, with listed amendments through 2025/1872 and no 2026 amendment listed. This supports the limited distinction between the entrusting company and assessment of an individual actor's conduct and fault. It is not an individual finding of guilt or a complete general-part explanation.

[Current KPW, Dz.U. 2025 poz. 860](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/860/text/U/D20250860Lj.pdf), 61 physical pages, editorial date 28.07.2026, includes 2026/473. The [underlying act API, DU2001/1148](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2001/1148) confirms the listed July amendment and the earlier changes used by the compiled text.

- Article 17 §2, physical pages 6–7, includes work-act article 84 within the labour inspector's public-prosecutor remit. Article 95 §3, page 43, supplies the corresponding mandate-procedure authority.
- Article 96 §1a point 1, physical page 44, now states the general 5,000 zł PIP mandate ceiling. The original 473 article 6, physical page 14, also changes specified repeated-offence ceilings to 10,000 zł.
- An important correction to preparation 111: **article 96 §1af point 1, physical page 45, separately permits up to 10,000 zł for work-act article 84 cases when PIP or SG is public prosecutor**. This is a subject-specific provision, not only a repeated-offence rule. The author found it during the fresh complete source read; Peirce independently reopened the same current page and confirmed it. The authored module uses this specific rule rather than presenting the preparation's general 5,000 zł figure as the relevant maximum. This process ceiling still does not increase the selected article 84(6) substantive maximum of 3,000 zł.
- Article 97 §2–3, physical page 47: right to refuse a mandate and information about the amount, act, legal basis, right and consequences. Article 99, physical page 48: following refusal, the organ submits an application for punishment to the court. Refusal is distinct from declining to sign the PIP protocol and does not automatically extinguish the allegation.

No mandate was imposed in the fiction. No payment date, mandate appeal, court judgment, cumulative fine or particular individual's fault is invented. Future provisions outside the selected current passages are not projected into the September explanation.

## Completed fiction and limits

The example is separate from the accepted employer-duties A/B chronology. A Polish company directly employs an adult Indian worker for ordinary nonseasonal warehouse work, without an agency or an independent exemption. A June 2026 ordinary permit covers the employer, work and conditions for 17.08–31.12.2026. Lawful stay on a compatible Polish national work visa is separately stipulated for the period. The matching Polish contract is signed on 13 August, its copy is sent to the issuing wojewoda through PGP-UZC that day, the language obligations are fulfilled, and actual work starts on 17 August.

Control begins on 24 August. The protocol presented on 27 August records both the true absence of a receipt in the initially supplied folder and the disputed inference that transmission did not occur. On 28 August, before signing, the employer submits written, reasoned objections with the already existing sent message, contract and receipt. On 2 September the inspector checks the recipient, worker, attachment and 13 August date and documents the correction of that specific finding.

The three Polish fragments clearly identify their different authors and dates. They are fictional selected excerpts, not an official national form, a real case record or one mandatory combined document. The objections are timely without a weekend or service-channel assumption. Nothing is uploaded retrospectively in order to pretend the original obligation was met. The actual conclusion concerns timely transmission only. Other payments, employment duties, any sanction and permit/residence decisions are not resolved by that correction.
