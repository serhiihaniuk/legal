## Decisive legal distinctions

Work-act article 3 ust. 3 requires both the issued/registered work instrument and lawful stay on an enumerated basis. Its visa point names foreigners-act article 60 ust. 1 pkt 3–10, 12–13b, 17–21 or 23–24b. Tourist purpose is pkt 1 and is absent. A matching permit does not rewrite that purpose or extend the visa's stay period. The example rules out another independent work basis, so its conclusion is confined to this specific combination.

The permanent-residence contrast uses only Polish permanent residence under work-act article 3 ust. 1 pkt 6. The article 3 ust. 2 pkt 1 paragraph explains work within the named residence permissions at orientation level; it does not reproduce or newly verify all their eligibility and change rules. Article 2 pkt 2 lit. a–c treats illegal stay, an incompatible stay basis and absence of a required permit/declaration separately. The word required is retained.

The ordinary permit's conditions follow article 32. A user is included conditionally under ust. 2; workplace is not invented as a universal statutory field. Article 17 ust. 1 pkt 1 and article 2 pkt 2 lit. d retain permitted changes and the independent-basis qualification. The explanation does not teach the portal transmission procedure or a numerical change window.

Article 4 ust. 5 exempts only article 3 ust. 1 pkt 1–5 persons from its ust. 2–4. It is not a general exemption for everyone who does not need a work permit. The article 5 ust. 1 written-contract statement retains the corporate exception through article 6 ust. 1 pkt 2. Signing a contract does not itself supply a missing stay basis.
