# Foreign employment and assignment evidence: preparation

Prepared on 12.09.2026. This is a bounded source and document-identity review after the posting permit, A1 and foreign-register work. It does not accept a new guide, change the global legal baseline or close all foreign-employment evidence gaps.

## Recommended next scope

Add one focused guide for an employer's individual letter describing a worker's existing foreign employment and planned assignment to Poland. Proposed ID: `posting-assignment-letter`. Proposed title: `Pismo pracodawcy o skierowaniu pracownika do pracy w Polsce`. This is a descriptive title for the selected private document, not the name of a statutory national form.

The reader's question is what the employer actually confirms about the worker, the work already performed abroad and the proposed Polish assignment, and which assertions still need their underlying records. A useful page can explain the author and source of knowledge, existing facts versus plans, differences from the contract and permit, and a completed comparison. Do not create an additional generic employment evidence-set or separate empty pages for each schedule, instruction or project record.

The letter is one possible component of the evidence. Do not say every applicant must obtain a separate document with this title, that it replaces all records required by the regulation, or that its wording establishes a legally valid posting. A combined employer letter may describe several facts if its author can support them. The employer does not acquire power to change contractual conditions merely by issuing the letter.

## Existing destinations and their limits

Read the full `employment-contract.ts`, `work-location-confirmation.ts` and `work-organisation-evidence.ts` guides, the relevant `professional-experience-confirmation.ts` sections, current delegation and posting-permit explanations, and batches 75 and 76.

- `employment-contract` explains agreed terms, amendments and the difference between gross pay and actual payment. Its legal discussion uses Polish employment rules, the Polish residence/work application and employer annex. A foreign employment contract is not automatically governed by those rules. Before presenting this destination as a full explanation of the selected foreign contract, add a bounded applicable-law qualification and distinguish the foreign contract from the posting letter. That refinement is a separate proposed edit, not performed here.
- `work-location-confirmation` answers a family-case question about temporary work in another Polish city and the spouses' addresses. It deliberately avoids deciding whether that situation is legally a business trip or posting. Extending it into an inbound posting manual would blur its useful existing scope.
- `professional-experience-confirmation` concerns a previous employer's account of qualifying experience for Blue Card. It does not explain a current employer's decision to send someone to Poland.
- `work-organisation-evidence` is honestly an evidence-set about company agreements versus actual supervision. It can remain a conditional overview where organization is disputed. It is not the individual posting letter or foreign employment contract.
- The posting permit, PIP notification, A1 and foreign-register record have different issuers and effects. None establishes the complete history of actual foreign work. The proposed letter must not become another name for those documents.

## Official sources checked

1. [Work act, Dz.U. 2025 poz. 621, current compiled text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/621/text/U/D20250621Lj.pdf), art. 40 ust. 1 and 3, art. 41, physical pages 38–39. Existing foreign employment, actual work abroad and proposed posting are separate factual elements. The export-service and linked-entity branches must not be merged. The employer's letter does not itself establish the remaining pay, working-condition or representative requirements. Art. 41 uses numbered points, not numbered paragraphs.
2. [Attachment regulation, Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1629](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1629/text/O/D20251629.pdf), physical pages 2–3. Section 4 pkt 3 requires evidence of foreign employment, actual work abroad and intended posting. It does not prescribe one named employer certificate. Section 4 pkt 4–6 separately addresses company links, the export-service contract and other posting purposes; pkt 7 concerns art. 41. Section 8 ust. 1–3 distinguishes electronic documents/scans, originals on request and further evidence; ust. 8 governs the specified sworn Polish translation, with the travel-document exception. Effective 01.12.2025. A selected letter is an editorial illustration of evidence, not an added statutory attachment.
3. [Posting act, Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1682](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1682/text/O/D20251682.pdf), art. 14, physical pages 8–9, and art. 25–26, pages 12–14. Article 14 concerns an overall assessment of genuine activity and temporary work; its indicators are not an all-or-nothing document checklist. Article 25 separates the employment contract or equivalent document, time records and identifiable pay/payment records retained for inspection. Those records do not become universal initial attachments to the PIP notice. Article 26 supplies the corresponding third-country-employer application, subject to work-act art. 41 pkt 2–3. Do not import art. 25c's information duties for posting from Poland as the statutory field list for an Indian employer's inbound letter.
4. [Dolnośląski UW: posting work-permit procedure](https://www.gov.pl/web/dolnoslaski-uw/zezwolenie-na-prace-w-zwiazku-z-delegowaniem-cudzoziemca-przez-podmiot-zagraniczny-na-terytorium-rzeczypospolitej-polskiej), current public page reopened. Use the published procedure only within its stated scope. No authenticated application or national employer-letter layout was inspected.

A search also returned [Mazowiecki UW guidance about the 2022 regulation](https://www.gov.pl/web/uw-mazowiecki/nowe-przepisy-odnosnie-zezwolen-na-prace). Its apparent current search date does not make its old rules current. Do not copy its former legal references, 2022 system instructions or unrelated 30-day statement rule into the proposed guide. The current regulation above is the basis for the evidence distinction.

## Proposed completed specimen

Use a separate fictional employer H in India, worker Y and Polish service recipient J. Do not modify the accepted German exemption, Indian issued-permit or British register examples.

Suggested sequence for author review:

- On 03.08.2026 the first employer letter says only that Y has been employed since 01.02.2025 and is planned to work in Poland on 01–30.10.2026. It does not identify where Y actually worked before the assignment.
- On 05.08 the employer issues a clarification based on its named employment and work records. It confirms the employment relationship, describes work actually performed in India during May–July, and identifies the planned software service for J and the October period. Those three months are the selected available records, not a statutory minimum. The letter identifies the author and source of knowledge. It does not certify October as already worked.
- On 06.08 the preparer completes the comparison. The missing account of actual foreign work now has a specific source, while the assignment remains a plan. The example ends at preparation. It contains no submitted permit application, issued permit, completed Polish work or conclusion that all legal conditions are met.

The selected Polish illustrative letter fragment can show issuer/worker placeholders, date, existing employment, evidenced foreign work, source records, intended recipient, service, place and dates. Explain that a real foreign-language original and its translation remain distinct. Do not impersonate a sworn translator's certification or reproduce identifiers. The fragment is not a foreign-law contract or an official form.

Name the actual supporting records individually: foreign employment contract and amendments, the selected work records, the contract underlying the service, and any relevant issued permission. Do not require all possible payroll, tax, insurance, corporate and travel documents merely because they could help in a different case. Keep a date or source discrepancy visible until the employer explains it; no backdating or silent replacement of the first letter.
