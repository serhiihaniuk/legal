## Exact provisions read and used

Page numbers are physical PDF pages, counted from one.

- **Page 4, Article 14 §§ 1a–1b and § 2.** Paper/electronic form and the automated-document qualification prevent a universal handwritten-signature assertion. Oral handling requires the party-interest and no-contrary-provision conditions and the specified record. The module does not infer an electronic service channel from these form rules.
- **Page 5, Article 16.** Administrative finality concerns absence of an administrative appeal or reconsideration request; `prawomocność` additionally concerns inability to challenge the final decision in court. No court deadline or judgment is invented.
- **Page 14, Article 57 § 1.** The service day is excluded from a period in days. The selected endpoints are 24 August 2026, Monday, and 2 September 2026, Wednesday. Both appeals are physically lodged at the issuing authority before their endpoints; no postal or electronic preservation fiction is needed.
- **Page 24, Articles 104–105.** Decision on the whole or part of the merits differs from another way of ending the case in an instance. Mandatory discontinuance for objectlessness is separate from the qualified discretionary request by the initiating party. The text does not treat every evidence failure or withdrawal letter as automatic discontinuance.
- **Page 24, Articles 106–106a.** A legal provision must require the other authority's position. The clock begins with delivery of the request to that authority and allows a different legally prescribed period. The position is an appealable order. The meeting's early-convening condition belongs to the requested authority's application. Calling the meeting does not remove the obligation to address the delay complaint. The module does not turn the two-week expiry into automatic consent.
- **Pages 24–25, Article 107.** Result, factual/legal reasons, instruction and identity/authorization have separate roles. The factual-reasons rule includes evidence accepted and reasons for rejecting other evidence. Section 4 has the full-grant, disputed-interests and appeal-decision boundaries. Section 5 retains its express connection to existing statutory allowances concerning state security or public order; it is not a general security excuse invented by the author.
- **Page 25, Article 108.** All listed grounds remain subject to necessity. The national-economy heavy-loss ground is not rewritten as any company's commercial interest. The party-interest ground is exceptionally important interest, with the possible security order. A later grant of immediate enforceability takes an appealable order.
- **Page 25, Articles 109–110.** Ordinary written service and the qualified oral route remain separate. The durable-medium rule is for attachments to a paper decision and requires consent. Its unchanged-information and original-version-reproduction alternatives are preserved. Binding of the issuing authority is explained under Article 110 § 1, without equating service with finality. Article 110 § 2 belongs to silent settlement and is not independently re-explained here.
- **Pages 25–26, Articles 111–113.** Completion concerns the specified missing operative or remedy information, with the fourteen-day request and ex officio periods. The module ties Article 111 § 2 to the order under § 1b. Article 113 correction concerns obvious errors; explanation of doubts requires the party's or enforcement authority's request. Its separate complaint is distinct from appeal against the original decision. Article 112 protection is qualified by the party having followed the incorrect instruction.
- **Page 28, Articles 123–126.** The procedural-question rule retains the Code's possible exceptions. Conditional reasoning and delivery rules preserve the current references to `sprzeciw` without creating that remedy for every order. Appropriate application under Article 126 is not all decision rules unchanged. The module describes the particular categories receiving the extraordinary-review incorporation without pretending that the incorporation itself proves grounds for reversal.
- **Pages 28–29, Articles 127–130 and 134.** The full-grant reasons-omission exception is exact. Reconsideration before a minister/SKO, receipt of the last party's waiver, ordinary appeal route/period and the limits on mandatory detailed grounds are distinct. Article 130's stay and exceptions are not equated with finality. Article 134 is named only to explain the order category expressly included by Article 126.
- **Page 31, Articles 141–144.** Separate complaint requires a statutory basis, normally within seven days. Deferred challenge under Article 142 and lack of automatic stay under Article 143 remain separate. Article 144 was read for context; no additional appellate mechanism is claimed on its basis.

## Document identities

`administrative-decision` is the issued original act; `procedural-order` is the issued completion or correction act. `administrative-appeal` links only the actual appeal against the original refusal. `procedural-complaint` appears for the conditional separate complaint against the specified order, not as a document actually filed in the example.

`delivery-proof` and `dispatch-proof` are evidence sets. The example selects incoming service records and office acknowledgements for identified outgoing documents. The requests for completion and correction have no focused catalog guide and remain plainly named with typed legal references. They are not mislinked to a working case index, assessment or special residence filing certificate.
