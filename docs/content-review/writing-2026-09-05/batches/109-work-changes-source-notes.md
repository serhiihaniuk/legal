## Work act and amendments

Primary text: [current compiled work act, DU 2025/621](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/621/text/U/D20250621Lj.pdf). The PDF header is 17 June 2026 and includes the amendments 1794, 203, 473 and 734. Future wording annotations were read against actual commencement dates.

The [ELI metadata](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/621), freshly read in this task, returned status `obowiązujący`. Its changing-act relations listed 734 effective 20 June 2026, 473 effective 8 July 2026, 203 effective 5 March 2026 and 1794 effective 27 April 2026. No later changing act was listed. This is a scoped metadata observation, not a claim that every provision or implementing measure was reviewed.

Physical PDF pages and operative findings:

- Page 2, article 1(2): entry and residence are governed separately. The example's lawful stay is an explicit fact, not inferred from a pending work application.
- Article 3(1)(6–7): Polish permanent residence and long-term EU residence provide independent work access. The article 24 discussion does not equate expiry of an old permit with loss of every work basis.
- Pages 25–27, article 17(1)(3–5), article 18 and article 20: pay-adjustment duties remain separate. Article 18 covers its specified entity changes, undertaking transfer and a changed position name with unchanged duties. It does not cover an ordinary move to an unrelated employer or genuinely different duties. Article 20 supplies the seven-day post-event notice period.
- Page 28, article 21(1–3): the next-permit application must be made during the existing permit, for the same position or type of work, with no formal defects or timely cure. Old conditions continue until the next decision becomes final in the administrative sense. Periods of party-requested suspension are excluded. Paragraph 2 is a specific system-failure rule. Paragraph 3 extends the rule to the exact named residence applications, not every residence-card application.
- Pages 28–30, articles 22–25: revocation, expiry by law following a named residence grant and the obligation to perform work are distinct. Article 24(2) includes only its named temporary routes and the same Polish employer and position. Article 25(1) removes the performance obligation for the period without the stated access conditions; it does not expressly terminate every underlying contract automatically.
- Pages 34–36, articles 32–33: ordinary permit fields do not include a universal separate workplace field. Article 33(1) permits different work for at most 30 days in a calendar year only with the other permit conditions preserved and prior notice to the issuing wojewoda. Article 33(2) has separate exceptions for specified labour agreements, proportional full-time increase and the employment-contract/PIP decision mechanism. These are not interchangeable with article 18's notice.
- Pages 42–44, articles 49–51: article 49 has no numbered paragraphs. Its 30-day ceiling ends earlier on service of the starosta's decision and applies to its specified other-document or unconnected visa-free situation. Article 50(2), not paragraph 3, gives the registered seasonal-application certificate's fields, including workplace. Article 51 concerns seasonal-visa or visa-free entry connected with the registered application. Work begins under its mechanism after the required arrival statement and travel/entry documents are presented and lasts until service of the decision, excluding party-requested suspension periods. The certificate is not itself a visa or an independent entry right. Paragraph 4 has the office-closed-day exception.
- Pages 45–47, articles 56 and 58: article 56's other-work exception is for the same Polish employer, the specified citizenship, pay no lower than the permit and no temporary-agency work. Its 30 days are counted during permit validity, not per calendar year. Paragraph 2 has its own contract-change and hours provisions. Article 58(1–3) limits the extension route to the specified seasonal entry and preserves the nine-month calendar-year limit. Paragraph 4 reaches finality for the existing employer and old conditions; paragraph 5 covers the specified post-harvest-contract situation. Paragraph 6 concerns an employer who did not entrust seasonal-permit work immediately before filing and reaches service, at most 30 days, under the application conditions or better.
- Pages 49–51, article 62: the registered declaration has its own fields, including workplace; its scope is not copied from article 32.
- Pages 54–57, articles 68–71: article 69(3) does not itself repeat article 33's proportional-pay phrase, while article 68(1)(4) independently requires the corresponding pay adjustment. Article 71 requires at least three months of prior employment under an employment contract linked to the declaration, an application before its registered end date, the same employer and position, an employment contract and formal completeness or timely cure. Conditions cannot be worse than the declaration. The endpoint is permit issuance or refusal service, not finality. Paragraph 2's suspension exclusion, paragraph 3's specific outage rule and paragraph 4's narrower residence-and-work extension are kept separate.
- Pages 97 and 99, articles 102–103 and 109: old pending proceedings not concluded by final decision, previously issued instruments and electronic-channel transition have different rules. The article does not claim that an article 18 or 33 component is live solely because the statute names electronic filing. No authenticated portal action was performed.

The [PIP amendment, DU 2026/473](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/473/text/O/D20260473.pdf), article 8 on physical page 15, adds the particular employment-relationship decision under PIP article 11(1)(7a) to work articles 33(2)(3) and 69(5). The [ELI act record](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2026/473/ogl) and API entryIntoForce field give 8 July 2026. The article does not substitute a generic inspection letter or protocol for this decision.

The complete one-page [nationality regulation, DU 2025/1617](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1617/text/O/D20251617.pdf), sections 1–5, was reread. The current list for the selected seasonal exception is Armenia, Belarus, Moldova and Ukraine. It took effect on 1 December 2025. The regulation separately preserves qualified older Georgian declaration work and old rules in already initiated unfinished proceedings. The module's new Moldova example does not depend on either historical exception; the article is not a new full review of the declaration route.

## Residence routes and procedural endpoint

The [current foreigners-law compilation, DU 2025/1079](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1079/text/U/D20251079Lj.pdf) was directly read at the named route definitions:

- Article 114(1/1a), physical pages 143–144: temporary residence and work, including the specified desired-profession category.
- Article 126(1/3), page 155: the particular board role without shares and the separately named prokurent extension. Article 21(3)'s express list names paragraph 1; article 24 names paragraphs 1 and 3. The module preserves that difference.
- Article 127, pages 155–156: highly qualified employment/Blue Card.
- Article 137a, pages 162–163: long-term mobility of a Blue Card holder.
- Article 142(3), pages 182–183: the named corporate functions under the business route. The module identifies the route without importing its enterprise-income test into ordinary employment continuation.

The [current KPA text, DU 2025/1691](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2025/1691/text/T/D20251691L.pdf) was read at article 16(1), physical page 5, and articles 127a and 129, page 29. Administrative finality, service and filing an appeal are different events. A timely appeal does not change the special article 71 endpoint. Article 127a ties waiver finality to receipt by the issuing authority of the last party's waiver. Article 129 provides the ordinary 14-day appeal period and filing through the issuing authority. The [KPA base metadata](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/1960/168) still lists 769, effective 13 July 2025, as its latest amendment; no later 2026 amendment was listed.

## Completed fictional comparison

This is a separate selected example, not a change to batch 107's A/B notice history. X and Y are adult Moldovans directly employed by one Polish employer on employment contracts, doing unchanged non-seasonal industrial warehouse work from 1 May 2026. Their lawful compatible visa stay through 31 December is expressly stipulated. There is no agency, independent exemption, party-requested suspension, revoked prior instrument or other work bar.

X's ordinary permit and Y's April-registered declaration cover 1 May to 31 August. Both belong to the current 2026 regime. The employer filed formally complete permit applications on 20 August for the same respective positions and employment contracts. The actual conditions remained unchanged. Y had already exceeded three months under the employment contract before filing.

Both refusals were issued 4 September, properly served on the employer 7 September and timely appealed 9 September. Neither was final on 9 September and no waiver had been made. The employer continued X's old work under article 21. Y was withheld from work from 8 September because article 71's refusal-service endpoint had passed. Both appeals remain pending in the example. No appeal judgment, future permit grant, invented refusal grounds or automatic contract termination was added.

The twelve Polish table cells are an educational completed comparison, not an official form or inspection record. Documents are linked for their actual functions: issued permit, registered declaration, employment contract, filing proof, service proof, decision and appeal. A receipt is not evidence by itself that an application was formally complete. No universal attachment bundle was created.
