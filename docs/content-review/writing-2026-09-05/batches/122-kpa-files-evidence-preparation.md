## Reader question and actual gaps

The central question is: what does access to the authority's file reveal, and how can a party correct or complete the material without confusing a document's presence, its authenticity and the truth of the fact it describes?

The legacy module mostly gives instructions to build a matrix and inspect files. Its only example says that a contract and unspecified ZUS data have different work dates, then tells the reader to investigate. It supplies no actual ZUS record, field, dates of observation, explanation or completed result. A registration date, reporting period and actual start of work are different facts; this example cannot be completed by choosing whichever date looks authoritative.

The rewrite needs substantive explanations of these distinctions:

- `Akta sprawy` are the authority's case materials. A party's working index helps locate records but is neither the authority's file nor proof that every listed attachment was filed. The current title's Ukrainian word "Акти" also risks suggesting individual legal acts; "Матеріали справи й докази" would state the topic more clearly while retaining the Polish title and stable ID.
- Ordinary inspection, notes and copies under article 73 differ from authenticated copies justified by an important interest. Access continues after the proceeding ends. Electronic access is an available mechanism, not a promise that every file is online.
- Access restrictions have exact grounds and an appealable-order form. A generic reference to an "internal document" is not itself the statutory ground. The complainant-data exception is narrower than hiding the whole file.
- A lawful source that may help explain a fact is admissible under article 75. Admission does not establish its truth, and an official document establishes what was officially attested within the authority's competence. Copy certification confirms correspondence with the original, not every factual assertion in a private contract.
- Article 77 imposes comprehensive collection and examination on the authority. A party can identify a missing source or contradiction. Article 78 requires granting a relevant evidence request, not merely considering it. Its second paragraph must not become a blanket late-evidence bar.
- The seven-day notice under article 79 concerns witness, expert and inspection evidence. It is not a universal deadline for documents or comments. Article 79a concerns proceedings initiated on request, party-dependent conditions unmet or unproved on the day the information is sent, and the specified risk of an adverse decision. It does not describe every possible refusal ground.
- Article 80 requires assessment of the entire material. Article 81 protects the opportunity to comment, subject to its exception. Article 81a applies to irreducible factual doubt in the specified obligation/restriction/removal proceedings, with all statutory exceptions; it is not a general right to receive a new permit despite missing proof.

The new text should not make every evidence issue a formal defect under article 64. That distinction is already explained in the preceding filing material and the accepted evidence-matrix article. Here a concise connection is enough.

## Proposed completed example

Use a separate ordinary KPA proceeding begun in August 2026. The party is in Poland and acts personally. There are no restricted records, disputed standing, foreign-language materials, personal summons or special application-channel rules in the example. The authority is investigating the agreed working-time term from 1 August as one relevant fact; this is not a claim about a universal residence condition, lawful work or actual hours performed.

A signed employment contract states 20 hours weekly. A signed annex dated 25 July changes that term to 40 hours from 1 August; the contract and annex's validity and authorship are undisputed in the fiction. No salary amount, permit decision or actual attendance is selected.

On 5 August the party files a covering letter listing the contract and annex through an allowed paper channel. The retained office mark confirms acceptance of the covering letter, but does not independently itemise or verify the attachments. The party cannot reconstruct the first packet's complete contents. Do not stipulate that the annex was actually included and then invent the authority's loss.

After an access request, on 17 August the party inspects the complete available file on the authority's premises with an employee. It contains the contract and covering letter, but the annex cannot be located. Ordinary copies are made; no important-interest justification or certified copies are required for that inspection. The private index is corrected from "annex filed 5 August" to "listed in covering letter; original submission not established".

On 18 August the party submits a signed evidential request with another complete copy of the annex. To avoid making an ordinary copy automatically equivalent to a certified one, the example can expressly say that the party also presents the original plus copy and requests actual certification by an authorised employee under 76a(2b). This is an expressly selected action, not a universal requirement for every annex. The receipt for this new submission identifies the annex. The original is returned; the authenticated copy remains in the file.

On 21 August the party's further inspection confirms that the annex is present. Read together, the two records now support the agreed change to 40 hours from 1 August. The completed outcome is the filled evidential gap and a correct distinction between the old and amended contractual term. It does not prove actual hours, pay, work authorisation or entitlement to a positive merits decision. It also does not retroactively prove whether the annex accompanied the 5 August letter. No merits decision, deadline extension or sanction is invented.

## Destinations and remaining verification boundaries

`employment-contract` honestly covers reading the selected employment contract with its signed annex. The two are distinct records even if they share a guide. `case-file-index` fits the private comparison; `dispatch-proof` fits each actual acceptance record, with its limited content. `procedural-order` and `procedural-complaint` are conditional links for an actual access refusal, absent from this fiction. Do not use those destinations for an ordinary access request or evidential request. No dedicated catalogue guide for either request was established here; plain record names and typed KPA references are sufficient.

Keep the following source limits visible in future implementation:

- This preparation does not renew Polish labour-law contract-validity rules. The selected signed agreement is stipulated; expanding into mandatory working-time terms or lawful changes would require fresh applicable labour/work-law verification.
- The old ZUS example is not accepted. If retained instead, name the actual ZUS document and field and freshly verify its meaning with ZUS sources. Generic "дані ZUS" is insufficient.
- The accepted maps explain the foreigners-law article 10 exception for a person abroad without a Poland-resident case representative. This preparation has not freshly verified that act's full current amendment chain. Either retain a general special-law boundary or perform that narrow verification before repeating the specific exception with a new date.
- Article 73(1b)/236 can be explained from the freshly read KPA text. A fuller GDPR-rights comparison requires its own relevant primary-source check.
- Do not label an article 79a communication as a wezwanie automatically. Its conditions, sending-date reference and selected comment period require their own explanation. The completed missing-annex example does not establish an impending adverse-decision procedure.

A suitable structure is access and copies; sources and the fact each can establish; collection and party requests; participation and pre-decision information; whole-material assessment and bounded doubt rules. The example can sit with collection rather than become a separate checklist. Preserve the core 73–81a panel and add no unrelated commentary range merely to increase coverage. No shared schema change is proposed.
