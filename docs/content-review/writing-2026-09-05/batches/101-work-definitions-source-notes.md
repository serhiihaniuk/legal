## Source distinctions retained

Work-act article 2 pkt 4 requires a contract or other legal relationship. An economic beneficiary is not automatically the entrusting entity. Pkt 5 combines the absence of the listed Polish territorial connections with foreign seat/permanent residence and business activity there. Pkt 6 expressly covers the Polish branch/representation and separately the EEA-citizen fixed-business-place alternative. Foreign ownership alone does not satisfy or displace those tests.

Temporary-workers article 2 pkt 1 concerns the agency-referred worker's tasks and control, while pkt 2 concerns the agency's employee. Article 7 distinguishes fixed-term employment from referral under a civil contract. Article 25a ust. 1 applies only the specified provisions appropriately to the latter. The module does not equate every civil contractor with an employee or every presence at a client's premises with agency referral.

Work-act article 2 pkt 9's corporate clauses retain presence in Poland and the specified registered entities/functions. Mere share ownership is not a listed corporate function. Article 6's permit categories do not override the selected independent work entitlement under article 3 ust. 1 pkt 6. The example expressly uses a Polish permanent residence permit. Article 4 ust. 5 exempts only article 3 ust. 1 pkt 1–5 persons from its ust. 2–4, so that exemption does not include the selected worker. The written-contract corporate exception and the distinct language rules are retained.

The two illegal-work definitions are not identical. Article 2 pkt 2 additionally covers conditions/position and written-contract issues with its express qualifications. Pkt 3 does not mechanically repeat these additional cases. The article preserves the required-permit qualification, legally allowed changes, other access bases, the article 119 qualification and the corporate written-contract exception. It does not assign an offence, fine or automatic liability to both actors.
