## Gaps in the existing module

The module mostly gives instructions to classify, count and formulate. Its example stops before any date calculation, actual filing, forwarding or appellate outcome. Replace this with authored sections and a completed example. Remove the question/exercise layer from the reader-facing explanation using the established authored-section contract, not a shared renderer redesign.

Substantive gaps to resolve:

1. Explain the act and available remedy before the writing technique. Ordinary first-instance decisions, the precise 127 §1a exception, reconsideration under 127 §3, separately appealable postanowienia and final admissibility orders are different situations.
2. Separate the statutory minimum in 128 from useful reasoned objections. A detailed list of legal errors is not universally a formal requirement. Keep the actual requirements of 63–64 and any special rule distinct.
3. Explain service, filing and later transmission as separate events. The party's 14 days under 129 and the first authority's seven days under 133 are not the same clock. Do not say filing directly with the appellate authority necessarily forfeits the remedy; discuss the statutory route and preserve the separate 65 §2 protection without promising the result of every mistaken filing.
4. Explain 109–113 without implying that any request for correction or clarification restarts the appeal period. The express new starting event in 111 §2 concerns the specified postanowienie. The ordinary obvious-error mechanism in 113 has no equivalent general restart rule. Erroneous instructions under 112 protect a party who followed them, not every mistake regardless of conduct.
5. Describe the current autokontrola powers under amended 132 and the subsequent appeal from its new decision. Do not preserve only the vague old formula "changes or revokes".
6. Give 134, 135 and 136 their separate jobs: admissibility/timeliness, possible suspension of immediate execution, and supplementary or expanded fact-finding. The 136 §2–4 route requires its actual party-agreement conditions and retains the excessive-difficulty qualification.
7. Distinguish every relevant 138 outcome. Discontinuing the appeal is not the same as revoking the decision and discontinuing the first-instance proceeding. A remand under §2 needs a procedural violation and an unresolved scope materially affecting the outcome. It is not automatic after discovering one omitted attachment. Explain §2a, §2b and the limited official-form case in §4 without making them the opening lesson.
8. Add current 139a. After a remand the first authority must consider the identified circumstances and follow the interpretative directions, subject to the statutory changed-law exception. The later appellate response in §2 is conditional, not an automatic sanction or permission to dictate a positive permit decision.
9. Separate withdrawal of an already filed appeal under 137 from waiver of the right under 127a. The former has a legal/public-interest safeguard; the latter takes effect upon receipt of the last party's declaration and changes both ostateczność and prawomocność.
10. Explain execution separately from existence of a substantive right. The ordinary 130 protection has exceptions, and 143 does not automatically suspend an appealed postanowienie. A suspended refusal is not a granted permit or independent work authorization.
11. Do not describe WSA as another administrative instance. Include the narrow distinction between ordinary judicial complaint and sprzeciw against a remand, including current PPSA 64f for remand postanowienia. Detailed court pleading belongs to the later court module.

## Proposed article structure

Six or seven connected sections are justified by this scope:

1. What is being challenged and who reviews it: ordinary odwołanie, exceptions, zażalenie and reconsideration.
2. A legally filed appeal: expression of dissatisfaction, identity/signature/channel, service and the 14-day period, representation and the precise effect of 111–113.
3. What filing changes: ordinary execution rules, notice to other parties, autokontrola and transfer, admissibility and restoration.
4. How the second instance establishes facts and decides: 136 and the different 138 results, remand directions under 139a, and the qualified prohibition of worsening the appellant's position.
5. Waiver and withdrawal: different documents, events and consequences.
6. Zażalenie in its own right: express enabling provision, seven days, appropriate application of appeal rules and no automatic suspension.
7. Completed document comparison and the boundary with judicial review. This can be integrated into section 6 if it reads better.

Keep explanations self-contained. Related links should deepen a point rather than replace its explanation. Do not impose a universal checklist requiring a particular number of objections, documents or attachments.

## Completed example selected with the parent

The existing appeal guide and map share a 17 August service / 28 August appeal / insurance-payment attachment example. Their completed result is filing and transfer, not a decision of the second instance. The existing two-instance map has a July refusal followed by an August decision upholding it. The new fiction is separate from both.

Proposed explicit premises and completed events:

- An ordinary KPA proceeding was initiated in 2026. There is one party, no representative, no waiver, no special filing period and no immediate execution. This is a simplified procedural hypothetical. Its premise is that actual use of specified premises in an identified period is relevant under the substantive rule applicable to that case. It does not assert that every residence permit requires actual residence at a leased address.
- The file contains a lease and the party's explanation. There is a concrete dispute about actual use, not merely about whether a lease exists. The party had requested examination of a named witness on key handover and use, and an inspection relevant to the disputed premises. Neither central evidence branch was conducted or meaningfully assessed. The refusal relies on an unsupported conclusion about non-use. Describe the relevance and omissions concretely; do not imply that any rejected evidence request automatically requires remand.
- The first-instance refusal was issued on 27 August and properly served on Monday 31 August 2026. The party filed a signed odwołanie through the issuing authority on Friday 4 September, received that day and evidenced by a receipt stamp. The appeal disputes the unsupported factual conclusion and the omitted investigation, and seeks reversal. It contains no request under 136 §2 for the appellate authority itself to conduct the necessary full investigation.
- The ordinary 14-day period excludes 31 August, begins on 1 September and ends Monday 14 September. Filing on 4 September is timely. The first authority did not use autokontrola and forwarded the appeal with the file on Thursday 10 September, within seven days of its 4 September receipt. The forwarding clock is not the party's appeal clock.
- On Thursday 17 September the appellate authority issued a decision under 138 §2 revoking the first-instance decision in full and returning the case for reconsideration. Its reasons identify the procedural violations, the central unresolved fact-finding and its material effect on the disposition. They explain why the necessary scope is not merely supplementation of a substantially established record under 136 §1. There is no agreed 136 §2/3 request, but its absence alone is not the reason remand is lawful.
- The appellate decision identifies the circumstances to establish on reconsideration: actual use in the legally relevant period, what the witness knows and how, and what the inspection and other records can establish. It does not direct the first authority to grant the application or predetermine witness credibility. If interpretation directions under 138 §2a are included, state the exact interpretative issue and source; they need not be invented merely to fill the example.
- The remand decision was properly served on Monday 21 September. The completed result is the actual revocation and remand. No new first-instance outcome, positive permit, automatic lawful stay/work conclusion or court judgment is established. No sprzeciw has been filed in the example. Explain the available remedy against that remand separately.

Calendar independently checked: 31 August, 14 and 21 September 2026 are Mondays; 4 September is Friday; 10 and 17 September are Thursdays. No weekend/holiday extension changes the selected 14-day appeal period. If the author gives a sprzeciw deadline, 14 days from service on 21 September ends Monday 5 October; that is a future deadline as of the review date, not a completed event.

The example remains expressly hypothetical as to the underlying substantive authorization. It is not an invented extract from a real decision or a source for a universal housing condition.
